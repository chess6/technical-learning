Spine row (M2 `calculus-technique`, L8 — the module's last lesson): *"An integral over an infinite interval is a **limit of finite accumulations**; convergence is a question about **decay rate**. Licenses every transform integral in the course."* Benchmark bar (M2, `benchmark-matrix.md` §2): standard improper integrals at P1; *improper convergence judged by decay rate* at P2; comparison and limit-comparison **proved**, plus conditional convergence, sit in the P3 enrichment column — used honestly here, proved there. Spine §10 calls L8 "the quiet keystone: without improper integrals every transform in M5–M7 is unlicensed notation."

## 1a. Diagnose the cognitive obstacle

**Primary: an incorrect prior model, installed by misleading notation.** \(\int_a^\infty f\) is typographically the object L3/L4 defined, so it reads as *"the same thing, with a bigger interval"* — already meaningful, already "the area," with \(\infty\) as just another endpoint. Two entangled halves: **(i)** the improper integral as a *completed infinite act* — symptom: writing \([F(x)]_1^\infty\) and "plugging in" \(\infty\); sharp failure: running the FTC ritual across a singularity, \(\int_{-1}^{1} x^{-2}dx = [-1/x]_{-1}^{1} = -2\), a **negative area for a positive integrand**, produced fluently and without alarm. **(ii)** *"the integrand goes to 0, so the total is finite"* — height confused with accumulated area, assigning \(1/x\) and \(1/x^2\) the same fate. A learner holding (i) cannot say what "fails to exist" would even mean; in their model the symbol is an object, not a claim. The notation is *shorthand for limits* that looks exactly like L3/L4's endpoint notation — the difficulty is notational/representational, so 1c is triggered.

**Not the obstacle:** computing antiderivatives (L7), or evaluating \(\lim_{R\to\infty}F(R)\) for explicit \(F\) (L1). The failure is in what kind of *claim* the improper integral is, and in what decides it.

**Costs if unrepaired:** L10 re-memorizes the identical demotion ("an infinite sum is the limit of its partial sums") as a second test-bag; every Fourier and Laplace integral in M5–M7 stays unlicensed notation; L24's region of convergence — *which \(s\) make the tail converge* — reads as bureaucracy; L11's radius of convergence loses its model.

## 1b. Raw leads

1. **The scandal.** \(\int_{-1}^{1} x^{-2}dx = -2\) by blind FTC. Nothing in the arithmetic is wrong; the *license* was missing — L3 guarantees the proper integral for a continuous integrand on a finite closed interval, and L4's FTC evaluation uses the same continuity hypothesis. Here the interval is finite and closed, but the integrand fails continuity at \(0\). The definition arrives as the restoration of a revoked license, not as pedantry. *(Counterexample + predictive.)*
2. **The demotion.** \(\int_a^\infty f\) is not a new object but a *claim about the family of honest L3/L4 integrals* \(\int_a^R f\) — that their values settle. \(F(\infty)\) is shorthand for a limit (\([\arctan x]_0^\infty\) works exactly when the limit does); both improper types are one move: exhaust the bad edge, ask whether the values settle. *(Predictive reorganization + structural compression.)*
3. **The confrontation.** \(1/x\) vs \(1/x^2\) on \([1,\infty)\): indistinguishable to belief (ii) — both die — yet \(F(R)=1-1/R\to 1\) while \(\ln R\) exceeds every bound. On the accumulation graph, convergence is *visible* as leveling off; the area picture shows nothing. *(Contrast + representational.)*
4. **Slowing is not settling.** \(\ln R\) grows ever more slowly *and* crosses every ceiling: name any \(M\), \(R=e^{M+1}\) defeats it — divergence as the mirror of L1's tolerance game. *(Predictive + interactive.)*
5. **The octave reveal and the ruler.** \(1/x\) pays *equal rent forever*: \(\int_{2^k}^{2^{k+1}}dx/x = \ln 2\), provable by one L7 substitution (scale invariance); \(1/x^2\)'s octave payments halve, trapped under a finite geometric bound. This is *why* \(p=1\) is the knife-edge. Sweeping \(p\) derives the whole ladder — \(\int_1^\infty x^{-p}\) converges iff \(p>1\), mirrored at a singularity (\(\int_0^1 x^{-p}\), iff \(p<1\)). *(Structural compression + representational.)*
6. **Convergence lives in the tail.** \(\int_a^\infty f = \int_a^c f + \int_c^\infty f\): the head is a proper integral — finite, owned, irrelevant. Move \(c\); nothing about convergence changes. *(Structural compression.)*
7. **The refusal.** \(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,dx\): symmetric truncations \([-b,b]\) report \(0\) forever; offset truncations run away. An "answer" that depends on the route is no answer — the two-independent-limits definition exists to *refuse* route-dependent values. *(Counterexample; purpose exposed.)*
8. **Divergence without infinity.** \(\int_0^\infty \sin x\,dx\): the accumulation oscillates in \([0,2]\) forever — bounded, never settling. "Diverges" means *the limit fails*, not "the area is infinite." *(Counterexample.)*
9. **Comparison as trapped accumulation.** If \(0\le f\le g\) on the tail and \(\int g\) settles, \(F(R)\) is a nondecreasing accumulation under a ceiling, so it settles — convergence decided **without the antiderivative**. The hinge ("a bounded nondecreasing accumulation converges") is a completeness principle to be *named* as the supplied hypothesis, exactly as L6 named the EVT. *(Operational + structural.)*
10. **The redemption of \(e^{-x^2}\).** L7 cited Liouville's result that no elementary antiderivative exists. L8 proves the *total exists anyway*: \(e^{-x^2}\le e^{-x}\) on \([1,\infty)\). Existence vs computability of the total — L7's central distinction, promoted one level. *(Predictive + continuity.)*
11. **"Goes to 0" is a survivor's condition.** For the decreasing positive integrands this module runs on, decay to 0 is necessary — and certifies **nothing**: L6's filter grammar verbatim ("it goes to 0" survives the filter the way \(f'(a)=0\) survived L6's). And it is *not* necessary in general (spike trains) — see the guardrails in 1e. *(Structural compression, cross-lesson.)*
12. **The boundary term at infinity.** Parts on \([0,R]\), then \(R\to\infty\): \(\int_0^\infty xe^{-x}dx = [-xe^{-x}]_0^R + \int_0^R e^{-x}dx \to 0+1\). The \([uv]\) entry at \(\infty\) is *itself a limit that must converge* — checked, never plugged. Theme 1's boundary term, and the exact shape of M7's Laplace derivative rule. *(Structural; Theme 1 continuation.)*

**Anti-anchoring note.** The spine's own sentence is treated as an inherited hypothesis, and read as universal it mildly **overclaims**: decay to 0 is neither sufficient (\(1/x\)) nor necessary (spike trains) for convergence of \(\int^\infty f\) — unlike the series case L10 meets, where \(a_n\to 0\) *is* necessary. The honest repair, which the lesson ends on: **convergence is a property of the tail; among the decaying comparison scale, the rate decides; comparison is how one tail's verdict is inherited by another.** As with L6, repairing the spine's wording is itself part of the lesson's content.

## 1c. Conventional vs. alternative presentation

**Conventional:** state \(\int_a^\infty f = \lim_{R\to\infty}\int_a^R f\) as a formality on line one (immediately re-abbreviated to "plug in \(\infty\)"), state the Type II analogue, state the \(p\)-test and comparison, drill — often with "check the integrand \(\to 0\)" taught as a first step.

**Alternative (selected direction):** run the learner's own ritual off a cliff first — the \(-2\) scandal — and let the violated hypotheses force the definition as a *repair*; make convergence visible on the accumulation graph (settling vs. creeping, the challenge game for divergence); derive the ladder with its mechanism (octave rents), not as a table; let comparison arrive as trap-under-a-ceiling on the redeemed \(e^{-x^2}\); run the route-dependence trap live so the two-limit rule is the only honest option. **Preserved:** the definitions (both types), the \(p\)-test, comparison with its hypotheses, every worked value. **Easier to infer:** why the definition has its shape; when the plug-in shorthand is legitimate; why decay alone decides nothing; why splitting \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty\) is mandatory. **Background introduced:** L1's limit grammar, L3/L4's proper-integral hypotheses, and L7's techniques; L8 derives the finite geometric bound and interval additivity it needs. **Transfer:** L10 has the same limit-of-finite-objects definition shape; L24's region of convergence is an architectural, not theorem-level, correspondence.

## 1d. Ranking (provisional, for Gate 4 to confirm or refute)

Consolidated packages (shared engines — the accumulation graph and the counterexample set serve all of them — are not counted as rivals): **P1, the demotion** (leads 1, 2, 6, 7, 8): there is no infinite integral — only finite ones and the claim that they settle; the claim lives in the tail; route-dependent readings are refused. **P2, rate not decay** (3, 4, 5, 11): the race is decided by *how fast* the integrand dies, calibrated by the \(p\)-ruler; "goes to 0" filters, never certifies. **P3, comparison** (9, 10): convergence knowable without the antiderivative; the \(e^{-x^2}\) redemption. **P4, the boundary term at infinity** (12): a component riding on P1's discipline, not a rival model — kept as a named beat.

Ranked **P1 > P2 > P3 > P4**. P1 is load-bearing: without the demotion, "does it converge?" is not a well-posed question — the learner has no account of what failure would be, which is why belief (ii) survives correct computations. It owns the topic's highest surprise (the scandal) and its strongest compression (both improper types, the bounds shorthand, the split rule, and the plug-in rule's domain of validity fall out of one sentence about L3/L4's contract). P2 is the benchmark bar's own phrase and the lesson's screen-time center, ranked second for *dependency*: the race is only runnable once the runner is \(F(R)\). P3 consumes both (settling as the question; the ruler as ammunition), and its hinge fact is a naming decision Gate 4 must settle. The spine's own sentence does not lead because it is P1+P2 stated in P2's vocabulary and, read cold, overclaims (see the anti-anchoring note); P1 first is what makes it safe to say. Correctness is a gate, not a tradeable score.

Selected insight candidate: **"In this course's improper-Riemann setting, the infinity notation names no completed infinite object; it abbreviates a claim that proper finite accumulations settle as their intervals exhaust the domain. That claim lives in the tail. For nonnegative tails compared with the \(p\)-family, decay rate decides the verdict, and comparison can inherit it even when no elementary antiderivative can be written. This supplies the notation later transform integrals use; each transform still owes its own convergence hypotheses."**

Why it survives the obvious attacks: it is falsifiable beat-by-beat (the scandal is a checkable two-line computation; every ladder rung is the learner's own FTC calculation; the octave equality is one L7 substitution; the \(e^{-x^2}\) claim rests on L7's cited non-elementarity result plus one inequality); it determines the media (the accumulation graph with a sliding right edge — L4's family with new data, **no new visual family**, supporting tier — plus the challenge game, the \(p\)-sweep, octave-bar overlays, and a truncation explorer for the refusal, the one candidate for new interaction surface, to be justified against the visual budget at Gate 5); and it gives the exercises their evidence shape (three-way classification with a produced comparator — see the evidence note in 1e).

**Evidence that would have flipped the ranking:** (1) if L4 had not shipped the accumulation function as a first-class graded object, P2 — runnable on FTC computations alone — would be the cheaper primary; L4 ships it by name. (2) If L3/L4 had not made their hypotheses explicit and graded, the scandal would be an assertion rather than a discovered repair, and the confrontation would be the stronger opener; they are explicit. (3) If the benchmark's P2 bar demanded *proved* comparison, the completeness hinge would become a real proof obligation and a P3-first split would deserve reconsideration; the bar places proofs in the P3 enrichment column, so naming suffices.

**Discovery sequence** (discover, not tell; exit is predict-not-recall):

1. The scandal — compute \(\int_{-1}^{1}x^{-2}dx=-2\) by the fluent L4 ritual; find the violated clause.
2. Rebuild honestly on \([1,R]\): slide \(R\), watch \(\int_1^R e^{-x}\) settle at 1; define the improper integral as the settled value — L1's guarantee with the window replaced by a threshold (the course's first limit at infinity, declared as new; L6's discipline holds: the limit supplies SOME threshold, never a rate).
3. The confrontation — predict \(1/x\) vs \(1/x^2\), then watch one level off and one creep.
4. The challenge game — slowing is not settling; divergence as an honest, checkable claim.
5. Octave bars and the \(p\)-sweep — derive the ladder, both edges, knife-edge to the losers.
6. Tails — split at \(c\), move it, nothing about convergence changes.
7. The refusal — symmetric vs offset windows on \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty x\,dx\) force the two-independent-limits definition.

**Abstraction return:** the race/rent vocabulary adds agents and completion — precisely misconception (i) re-armed — so Gate 4's Audit B5 must name and discard it with unusual care.
